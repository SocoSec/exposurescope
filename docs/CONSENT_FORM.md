## Part 1 — What this study involves

**What you'll do.** You'll use a research tool called Exposio to check **your own** email address
against public records of known data breaches. You'll then answer some questions about what you
saw and how easy it was to use. The session takes about **25 minutes**. Two weeks later you'll
receive **one short follow-up questionnaire** (about 4 minutes).

**What you'll see.** Exposio shows which known breaches include your address, a score from 0 to
100 describing how exposed that leaves you, and a ranked list of reviewed steps you can take.

**This may be uncomfortable.** Some people find it unsettling to see their address listed in
breaches, particularly if they still use an affected password. The tool always ends with
practical steps, and the researcher is there to help you work through the first one. You can
stop at any point, for any reason, and you do not have to explain why.

**You will work on your own device, and the researcher will not look at your screen while your
results are shown.** You will be asked only for two figures from the summary — which band you
were in, and how many breaches were listed. You will never be asked to show or share the list
itself.

## Part 2 — What happens to your data

There are **two separate things** here, and they are handled differently.

### The tool stores nothing

| | |
|---|---|
| Your email address | Used for this one check and the code confirming it is yours. Never stored, sold, or added to any mailing list |
| Your results | Shown on your screen and gone when you close the tab. No database, no account, no history |
| Your password *(optional task)* | **Never leaves your browser.** Only 5 characters of its mathematical fingerprint are sent, which cannot be turned back into the password |
| The AI assistant | Receives your score, breached company names and exposed data types — **not** your email address |

Full detail is in the privacy notice at **/privacy**, which describes exactly what the software
does. The source code is public.

### The questionnaires do store your answers

Your questionnaire responses **are** kept, because they are the research data:

- Stored against a **participant ID**, not your name or email
- Held on _[university survey platform]_, within the EU
- The list linking your ID to your email (needed only to send the follow-up) is kept separately
  and **destroyed once the follow-up closes**
- Kept until the thesis defence plus one year, then deleted
- Reported only in aggregate or as anonymous quotes

**Your breach results are not collected.** The researcher records only the band and the count,
as you report them.

## Part 3 — Your rights

- Participation is **voluntary**. You may stop at any time, without giving a reason, and without
  any disadvantage to you or to your studies.
- You may skip any question or task — including the optional password check.
- You may withdraw your data up to the point it is anonymised for analysis. After that it can no
  longer be identified and removed.
- You may ask to see what is held about you, or ask for it to be deleted, by contacting the
  researcher at _[email]_.
- If you have concerns about how this study was conducted, contact _[supervisor / ethics committee contact]_.
