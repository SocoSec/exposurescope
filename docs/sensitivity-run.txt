2. OPERATIONAL MEANING OF THE CONSTANTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   λ = 0.002/day  →  half-life = ln2/λ = 347 days (0.95 years)
   A breach's contribution halves every 11.4 months.

   financial       sev 1 — alone reaches 100 if younger than 112 days
   financial-only  sev 0.7 — cannot reach 100 alone (sev < B_MAX)
   credentials     sev 0.7 — cannot reach 100 alone (sev < B_MAX)
   contact         sev 0.4 — cannot reach 100 alone (sev < B_MAX)
   email           sev 0.2 — cannot reach 100 alone (sev < B_MAX)

   B_MAX = 0.8 therefore defines the top of the scale as:
   "one payment-data-plus-password breach about 112 days old".
   This is the interpretation the thesis must state, because it is what
   chooses B_MAX: the constant is a definition of the ceiling, not a fitted value.

   band low/medium (36)    needs B_raw = 0.288  ≈ one credential breach 444 days old
   band medium/high (66)   needs B_raw = 0.528  ≈ one credential breach 141 days old

7. COMPARISON WITH ALTERNATIVE WEIGHTING SCHEMES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   model                      mean    ρ vs proposed   τ-b     band agreement
   M0 single clock (old)      22.6         0.750       0.616    50.4%
   M1 count only              38.9         0.742       0.569    62.5%
   M2 severity, no decay      51.7         0.774       0.583    63.4%
   M3 worst breach only       14.8         0.696       0.569    42.0%
   M4 linear decay (10y)      31.8         0.786       0.622    49.4%
   M5 binary credential       69.2         0.504       0.383    50.5%
   M6 log-count × severity    40.9         0.760       0.574    62.3%

   Reading: under the single-clock model M3 (worst breach only) correlated
   at rho = 0.966, tau-b = 0.880 - the sum added almost nothing over the
   maximum, so aggregation could not be claimed as a contribution. A union
   term cannot be recovered from any single breach, and M3 falls to the
   figures above. That drop is the evidence that aggregation does work.
   M0 is the previous model: its correlation with this one measures how
   much the two clocks changed the ranking, and it is far from 1.

8. WHERE THE SCORE COMES FROM: THE TWO CLOCKS, AND CONCENTRATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   permanent (slow clock) share of B_raw:   median 99.0%   mean 82.6%
   identities whose score is >50% permanent: 87.7%

   Within the credential half alone, concentration persists:
   top breach's share of B_cred:  median 100.0%   mean 90.3%

   Reading: the fast clock is still dominated by one recent breach, which is
   why the action plan ranks by it. The slow clock behaves differently - it
   is the part no action removes, and for most identities it is now the
   majority of the score. That is the claim the two-clock model makes.

9. WHAT ADDING SOURCES ACTUALLY BUYS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   More providers do not make the weights better; nothing here calibrates
   them. What they do is replace ASSUMED values with observed ones. The
   undated-age assumption is the clearest case: a record with no date is
   scored at 730 days because nobody knows better, and section 5
   shows how much that choice moves the result.

   Below, a fraction of the undated records have their real date recovered,
   as the bundled catalogue does for a breach another provider named. For
   each fraction the undated assumption is swept from 180 to 2920 days and
   the SPREAD in rank correlation is reported. A narrower spread means the
   answer depends less on a number nobody can justify.

   dates recovered   still undated   rho spread across the sweep
              0%           18.6%        0.047   <- no catalogue
             25%           14.0%        0.036
             50%            9.2%        0.027
             75%            4.5%        0.013
            100%            0.0%        0.000   <- nothing left to assume

   Reading: at full recovery the spread goes to zero, because there is no
   undated record left for the assumption to apply to. That is the honest
   account of what extra sources contribute: not a better model, but fewer
   places where the model has to guess. The same argument applies to data
   classes, where the vocabulary audit raised recognised classes from 30 to
   67 of the 166 published, and to corroboration, measured in
   scripts/confidence-effect.mjs rather than here.
