------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   reference: 1048 breaches fetched live from HIBP (keyless)

   B1  canonical titles resolving to the right date    1045/1048  (99.7 %)
       resolving to a date belonging to another breach 0  ✓

       mangling                 resolved        rate
       lowercased                 1045/1048     99.7 %
       spaces stripped            1045/1048     99.7 %
       underscored                1045/1048     99.7 %
       domain suffix added        1010/1048     96.4 %
       year appended              1010/1048     96.4 %
       'combo list' suffix        1010/1048     96.4 %
       'database' suffix          1010/1048     96.4 %
       '(leaked)' suffix          1010/1048     96.4 %

   Every transformation observed in provider output resolves at 96.4 % or
   better; the weakest is "domain suffix added". This matters because a name that fails
   to resolve leaves the record undated, and an undated record is scored on the
   model's most consequential assumption rather than on an observation.

   B3  fuzzy keys onto which >1 breach date maps      15
       fuzzy keys marked ambiguous, carrying no date  24
       "abandonia"  <-  Abandonia (2015) (2015-11-01) | Abandonia (2022) (2022-11-15)
       "adultfriendfinder"  <-  Adult FriendFinder (2015) (2015-05-21) | Adult FriendFinder (2016) (2016-10-16)
       "bell"  <-  Bell (2014 breach) (2014-02-01) | Bell (2017 breach) (2017-05-15)
       "breachforums"  <-  BreachForums (2022-11-29) | BreachForums (2025) (2025-08-11)

   A bare name that maps onto two breaches with different dates is resolved by
   the exact-name map where the provider quoted a canonical title, and refused
   outright where it did not. 24 of 1328 fuzzy keys (1.8 %) fall to the
   refusal, and are scored on the undated assumption with the reason recorded.
   A wrong date mis-scores silently; a missing one is declared, which is why
   refusing is the conservative choice even though it loses information.

C. QUALITY COMPOSITION OF THE REFERENCE CORPUS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       mark            breaches     share

       spam-list            16      1.5 %

       stealer-log           6      0.6 %
       (no mark)           973     92.8 %

   5.8 % of catalogued breaches carry a mark that qualifies their reliability.
   This is a property of breach intelligence as a field, not of this tool, and
   it is the quantitative basis for the source criticism in chapter 2: a score
   computed over such a corpus inherits its uncertainty, which is why the marks
   are surfaced in the report rather than silently folded into the number.

D. WHAT REMAINS UNMEASURED, AND WHAT IT WOULD TAKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   SENSITIVITY (false negatives). Measuring how often a source misses a record
   that is genuinely present requires an identifier set whose true membership is
   known. No provider publishes corpus contents, and reconstructing them would
   mean obtaining breach data directly, which is both unlawful and the opposite
   of what this architecture is for. Relative coverage between providers is
   measurable and is the closest available substitute; it bounds disagreement,
   not error, because when two sources differ neither is known to be right.

   CONSTRUCT VALIDITY of the score. Nothing here shows that a score of 70 denotes
   more real-world harm than a score of 40. That requires outcome data linking
   exposure to realised compromise, which does not exist publicly, and is the
   subject of the calibration discussion rather than of this script.

   USER COMPREHENSION AND BEHAVIOUR. Requires the controlled study specified in
   docs/STUDY-PROTOCOL.md, with ethics approval and recruited participants.

   The distinction this script draws is the one the thesis should draw: the
   measurement layer is testable and has been tested; the interpretation layer
   is not yet validated and is reported as such.
